The Hilbert-space step
For every finite word w of smeared fields, let v = w Omega and let
v0 = v - <Omega,v> Omega. A common positive decay exponent Delta for the
diagonal spectral Laplace transforms of v0 suffices. Each word may use its
own finite prefactor and its own sequence of times tending to infinity.
Positivity bounds the mass at 0 < energy <= Delta - epsilon by the Laplace
transform times exp((Delta - epsilon)t). The limit kills each closed
sub-strip and then the entire open strip. The vacuum has zero strip mass.
W1 identifies the dense domain with the complex linear span of word
vectors. The kernel of the bounded strip projection is closed, so that
projection vanishes on the full Hilbert space. The existing Lorentz boost
theorem then excludes sub-gap invariant masses, including massless nonzero
momenta. This proof uses W0/W1/W2 of the specified theory.

Finite-volume input
The observables are products of normalized traces of actual spatial
plaquettes under the pushforward of a periodic SU(2) Wilson measure.
For words of lengths m and n whose every cross pair is separated by at
least k coarse time steps on the torus, the absolute connected correlation
is bounded by mn * 4b pi^2 alpha^(bk)/(1-alpha). Repeated plaquettes and
multi-slice insertions are allowed. Here b is the straight-link block
factor, alpha = 18 beta/sqrt(3), and 0 <= beta < sqrt(3)/18.
Signed sums are bounded using sum_i |c_i| m_i n_i.

Continuum matching
The reconstruction's covers and represents fields provide actual Wilson
tokens for every continuum field label. The conjugated tests and reversed
adjoint product give the diagonal characteristic function as
    <Omega, w^* translated(w) Omega> - |<Omega,w Omega>|^2.
Each expectation has the public reconstruction's smeared boundary-value
formula. For a sequence of finite positive momentum measures, matching
characteristic functions at every real translation implies weak convergence
to the word's diagonal measure. The zero translation recovers the mass,
including zero-norm words. At nonnegative energy, the Euclidean kernel
agrees with the bounded continuous function exp(-t max(0,energy)).
Consequently every fixed nonnegative-time Laplace moment converges.

reconstructed_gap_of_translation_match additionally requires that those
finite measures' Laplace moments on the selected sparse shells are bounded
by actual signed Wilson product expansions with statewise bounded weights.
It outputs log(1/alpha_max)/R for fixed fine physical spacing R. Building
the finite measures with these properties, identifying their real-time
limits with the reconstructed field words, and establishing the continuum
theory are outstanding physical inputs.

Weak-coupling test
The public running assumption has a positive logarithmic slope uniformly
in the box index. Since the strictly increasing mesh denominators diverge,
beta tends to infinity even along changing boxes. For every sequence of
positive block factors and physical spacings the inferred bare-row rate
-log(alpha^b)/(b R) is eventually nonpositive. This constrains the
estimate and leaves the actual weak-coupling spectrum open.

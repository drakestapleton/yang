At fixed strength r, rotate the pole centers through an angle D on a circle
subgroup. Before that rotation, move backwards along the circle by the amount
b that sweeps out the unnormalized tilted mass D/cosh(r). On an orbit with
weight w(t), the backward shift S has derivative w(t)/w(S(t)). Changing
variables over a whole period and averaging over Haar proves that the shift
preserves the centered tilted law. The remaining displacement is bounded
pointwise by |D-b| <= tanh(r) D. Thus the map has the correct full tilted
marginal for any pole pair, including at zero strength and antipodal centers.

Compose this move with the common-pole coupling and use Wasserstein symmetry
to rotate at the smaller strength. The resulting root mean square cost is at
most |s-r|/sqrt(12) + tanh(min(r,s)) D. The Cartesian bound uses tanh(m) <= m
and the round arc-to-chord inequality. Its squared coefficient is 1/12 + pi²/4.

For periodic 4D SU(2) Wilson heat baths on every side l >= 2, an explicit
geodesic Wasserstein influence matrix has zero diagonal and row sum
18 beta sqrt(1/12 + pi²/4). Neighbor appearances are counted with
multiplicity. This matrix has row sum strictly below one at beta = 17/500.
No common-pole assumption, gauge fixing, or quadrature enters this bound.
The sharper transverse constant and the continuum shell comparison remain open.

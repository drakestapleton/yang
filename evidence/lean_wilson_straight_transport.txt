The geodesic cost is the squared great-circle distance on the unit 3-sphere.
Conditioning the second coupling on its first coordinate and composing it
with the first coupling recovers both original pair marginals. Integrating
the geodesic triangle inequality controls the cost of the outer coupling.
An epsilon limit allows couplings arbitrarily close to the infimum.

At a nonzero Cartesian staple field, the short angular arc divided by its
chord tends to one. The radial coefficient is 1/sqrt(12) and the transverse
coefficient is 1/2. Cauchy–Schwarz bounds their combined squared coefficient
by 1/12 + 1/4 = 1/3. At zero field there is only a radial cost. Closed-interval
induction along the straight segment, followed by the limit of the local
slack, proves the global squared Wasserstein bound for every pair of fields.

For actual periodic Wilson links the row is alpha = 18 beta / sqrt(3).
The random-scan updating operator uniformly chooses one of N links. The
Wilson disintegration proves that every site update preserves the Wilson
mean. The sum of the coordinate Lipschitz weights contracts at each update
by q = (N-1+alpha)/N. Uniform convergence of the sampler expectations to
the full Wilson expectation follows below sqrt(3)/18.

For spatial correlations, the factor of 1/N from averaging the site updates
cancels when the changes in a weighted observable are summed along the
sampler trajectory. Centering the second observable at a fixed value of the
updated link cancels its background term by stationarity. A nonnegative
profile p satisfying p >= w + A p therefore gives |Cov(F,G)| <= pi² sum v p.
Iterating from the constant profile B/(1-alpha) bounds p at the first support
by B alpha^k/(1-alpha) when the second support is k neighbor steps away.

A plaquette incidence changes the base-site coordinate by at most one in
each periodic direction. The shortest displacement is the absolute value of
the centered residue. Thus cyclic coordinate separation by k implies the
same separation in the incidence graph. The normalized real trace of a
Wilson plaquette is gauge invariant. It has weight one at each of its four
distinct links, each lying in the same slice for a spatial plaquette. Hence
for two such traces |Cov| <= 4 pi² alpha^k/(1-alpha), uniformly in side.
The Euclidean lattice separation k and the number of sampler updates are
different quantities in these proofs.

The continuum corollary takes a W0/W1/W2 theory, a positive physical shell
spacing R, and a representation of the spectral Laplace transforms of every
vacuum-orthogonal state by limits of separated periodic Wilson covariances
with a uniform Lipschitz prefactor for that state. It proves the covariance
inequality and passes it to the limit inside Lean. Constructing the spectral
representation along a continuum trajectory remains open. The all-direction
metric coefficient at beta = 0.192 also remains open.

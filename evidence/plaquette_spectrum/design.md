# Periodic plaquette measurement at β = 2.3

On a periodic 8³ spatial box, measure the unflowed fundamental 01 plaquette, with axis 3 as time. The operator P(t) is the spatial average of one half of the real trace. Form the product of two slice averages. Average over source times. At each temporal extent subtract the square of that extent's ensemble mean.

The pilot uses T = 8, 16, chain IDs 0–7, 160 warmup sweeps, 192 outer observations and eight subupdates. The production run uses the same extents, IDs 10–25, 256 warmup sweeps, 512 outer observations and 24 subupdates. Alternate cold and hot starts by chain ID. Separate outer observations with two full Wilson heatbath sweeps. Seeds depend on both extent and chain ID. The outer lattice and its PRNG have their own device buffers and the subupdates advance a separate PRNG. All sweeps update each link direction and checkerboard parity in turn.

Freeze spatial links on every fourth slice in production (every second slice in the pilot). Rotate the boundary offset by one per outer observation. Temporal links incident on the boundaries are dynamical. Check each frozen link bit for bit after the first subupdate of each observation. For d > 0, average products of conditional means at interior endpoints in distinct slabs when possible. Otherwise use a boundary endpoint and an interior endpoint. Translation invariance makes the selected origins equivalent in expectation. For d = 0 use the conditional second moment of the slice average. All eligible origins are averaged within an observation. Use the ordinary estimate at all origins.

The reduction subtracts a fixed numerical offset of 0.6 from each slice before forming products, then subtracts an unbiased estimate of the residual squared ensemble mean using products across distinct chains. Both estimators share the same one-point estimate. Uncertainties and estimator differences use a delete-one-chain jackknife, including recentering and recomputation of derived quantities.

Use these descriptive rules for production. A positive separation is resolved when C exceeds three chain standard errors. Where both estimators resolve a lag, check their paired difference against three paired standard errors. Report the periodic effective energy acosh((C(d−1)+C(d+1))/(2C(d))) only when all three lags resolve and the transform exists for the full sample and every deletion. Two adjacent energies give a candidate plateau when their difference, with a two-standard-error margin, lies within ±0.20 in lattice units. This tolerance describes the sensitivity of this experiment. Compare disjoint halves of the chain IDs for reproducibility, hot and cold starts for equilibration, and the shared lags at both extents for sensitivity to the temporal size. A lack of precision leaves a plateau undetermined.
